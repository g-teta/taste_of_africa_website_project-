Our table

A love letter to the food of Africa.

Tastes of Africa is an independent culinary journal devoted to recipes with roots, people with stories and the many ways food makes a place feel like home.

Why we’re here

Preserving tradition.
Celebrating evolution.

There is no single African cuisine. There are thousands, shaped by land, language, movement and imagination. We document dishes in their full context, with care for the people and places that made them.

Our recipes are designed to be cooked, shared and adapted in your own kitchen.

Our mission

To celebrate, preserve and share African food heritage, one recipe, one story and one table at a time.

History

A brief history of African cuisine

African cooking is thousands of years old. Ancient grains such as sorghum, millet and teff were farmed long before foreign crops arrived. Trade across the Sahara, the Nile and the Indian Ocean brought new spices, rice and citrus.

Later, maize, cassava, tomatoes and chillies arrived from the Americas and became staples of the continent’s kitchens.

African food also travelled with people. The foods and techniques of enslaved Africans shaped the cooking of the Caribbean, Brazil and the American South, and jollof is now served in restaurants around the world.

A food landmark

Makola Market, Accra

One of West Africa’s busiest markets, where traders sell fresh produce, spices and smoked fish from before sunrise.
